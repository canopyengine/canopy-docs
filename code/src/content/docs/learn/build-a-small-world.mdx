A game world starts with pieces: a fish, a button, or a group that holds other
pieces. Canopy calls a piece a **node**. You can put nodes inside other nodes to
organize your world.

You only need basic Kotlin to follow this page. The activities are optional,
require no installation, and do not execute Kotlin.

## Put the pieces together

Imagine a folder named Aquarium with two fish inside. Aquarium is the **parent**;
the fish are its **children**. This comparison describes how pieces are grouped.
It does not mean moving a parent automatically moves its children.

<NodePlayground />

The example uses `EmptyNode`, a node that groups pieces without drawing anything.
The fish in the illustration help you see the structure; they are not rendered
by those empty nodes.

## Give the group a scene

A **scene** is a tree of nodes with one root. Think of putting a set on a stage:
the engine can now enter it, update its pieces, and eventually remove it.
`aquarium.asSceneRoot()` makes Aquarium the root of the scene in the example.

To try the fragment in a real project, follow [your first project](/manual/manuals/getting-started/first-project/).
Replace that starter’s `onEnter` body with the fragment above and import
`io.canopy.engine.core.nodes.types.empty.EmptyNode`. Keep the starter’s App and
launch setup. This builds the tree; a visible aquarium needs drawing nodes and
game rules, which this short lesson does not add.

## Move forward one frame

The tree describes what is in your game. Rules describe what happens over time.
A frame gives the engine a chance to handle input, advance those rules, and
present the result.
